## 1. Risk register

**Likelihood:** Low means it would be surprising; Medium means it should be planned for; High means assume it happens.
**Impact:** Low means a day of rework; Medium means a phase slips; High means a gate cannot pass; Critical means the product cannot deliver its purpose or the business is exposed.

## 2. Rollback triggers

A rollback trigger is a condition under which a capability is switched off without debate. The default is off. Turning something back on requires evidence, not confidence.

| # | Condition | Flag or component | Action | Actor | Evidence required to reverse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RB-01 | Any prompt-injection case succeeds: a gate is overridden, the output schema is altered, or system text is echoed | `flag.llm_extraction`, `flag.llm_adjudication` | Disable all model flags immediately; deterministic-only until the case is fixed | Developer, no approval needed | The failing case is added to the injection suite, the suite passes 20 of 20, and the promotion record is re-signed |
| RB-02 | Model output changes a band on a lead where the operator later confirms the deterministic result was correct, and this happens 3 times in a rolling 50 leads | `flag.llm_adjudication` | Disable adjudication; keep extraction if it is independent and still within agreement | Developer | Agreement returns to at least 90 per cent over the next 50 leads |
| RB-03 | Model schema validation failures exceed 5 per cent of calls over 200 calls | `flag.llm_extraction`, `flag.llm_adjudication` | Disable the affected phase; the circuit breaker also opens automatically for 15 minutes | Automatic, then Developer | The schema or the prompt is fixed, and 200 consecutive calls validate |
| RB-04 | Monthly AI spend reaches 100 per cent of the cap | Active provider | Automatic fall back to `NullProvider`; no model calls until the next period | Automatic | The period resets, or an Administrator raises the cap with re-authentication |
| RB-05 | Draft fact-check pass rate falls below 100 per cent on any single day | `flag.drafter_v1`, per template | Disable the failing template; serve no draft for the affected channel rather than a wrong one | Automatic, then Developer | The template is fixed, every affected draft is regenerated, and 100 per cent of a 50-draft sample passes |
| RB-06 | Top-1 product-match accuracy falls below 85 per cent on the evaluation set | Matcher release | Do not ship the matcher change; revert to the previous matcher version | Developer | A new evaluation run passes at or above 85 per cent |
| RB-07 | Hard-gate precision falls below 100 per cent on the evaluation set | Policy release | Do not ship; revert the policy version | Developer | A new evaluation run passes at 100 per cent, and every false positive is reviewed |
| RB-08 | Any week shows `allocated_count` above 75, in test or in production | Allowance and ingest | Immediately pause ingest; do not process further batches until the cause is found | Automatic pause, Developer investigation | The concurrency test passes at 20 workers and the specific cause is written up |
| RB-09 | Any duplicate lead or duplicate allocation is found in production | Ingest | Pause ingest; audit the batch; re-run the dedupe suite | Developer | Zero duplicates in a full re-import of the affected data, and the constraint review is complete |
| RB-10 | Operator agreement falls below 90 per cent over a rolling 50 leads | Current `policy_version` | Revert to the last policy version that met agreement; treat the change as unproven | Developer, Office principal informed | The disagreement review is signed, the change is explained, and agreement recovers over 50 leads |
| RB-11 | A shadow phase has run for fewer than 50 leads or fewer than 3 days | Any model phase | Do not promote; remain in shadow | Automatic by the release checklist | Both limbs of the shadow duration are met, agreement is at least 90 per cent, and disagreements are reviewed |
| RB-12 | A backup fails, or the newest backup is older than 30 hours | Backup job | Alert; run an immediate backup; block the next release until a successful backup and restore exist | Automatic alert, Developer action | A successful backup plus a successful restore onto a scratch database |
| RB-13 | A restore onto a scratch database fails | Release process | Block any migration or release; fix the restore path first | Developer | A successful restore with matching row counts |
| RB-14 | Any plaintext buyer phone or email appears in a log, an export without permission, a bundle or an AI request | Redaction and PII controls | Disable the leaking path; rotate the PII keys; purge the affected artefacts | Developer, no approval needed | A redaction test suite covering the leaking path passes, and the rotation is recorded |
| RB-15 | The portal returns 401 or 403 that does not resolve within one working day | Source adapter | Mark the source `degraded`; switch primary to the CSV path; notify the Office principal | Automatic, then Office principal | The portal is fixed and one full pull reconciles against the dashboard count |
| RB-16 | The queue worker has failed to process any job for 15 minutes while jobs are pending | Queue worker | Restart the worker; if it fails twice, disable draft generation to keep ingest moving | Automatic restart, Developer escalation | Jobs drain, and the failed-job count is reviewed |
| RB-17 | Any lead reaches `ready` while a hard gate is fired | Policy | Stop the pipeline; treat as a critical defect | Developer | A regression test proves the gate cannot be bypassed, and a full evaluation run passes at 100 per cent gate precision |
| RB-18 | Aggregate agreement between the system band and the operator decision is below 70 per cent for any full week after the pilot | Whole scoring engine | Fall back to score-only ordering with no band labels, so the operator is not steered by an unvalidated classification | Developer, Office principal | The disagreement review is signed and the weights or the catalogue are corrected |

## 3. Open questions

Each question has a default assumption, so that a missing answer delays nothing. The default is explicitly conservative: it under-claims rather than over-claims, and it never assumes a business fact.

| ID | Question | Why it matters | Who answers | Needed by phase | Default assumption if unanswered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OQ-01 | What is the weekly fee for the 75-lead contract, and in what currency? | It is the only input to the derived unit cost. Without it, waste can be reported in units but not in money, and the operator's third-ranked pain "spend visibility" is only half answered. | Office principal | P4, before the allowance views are built | No figure is assumed. `weekly_fee_minor` is a configured input; until it is set, waste is reported in units only, every currency figure is labelled "derived, fee not configured", and no placeholder number is shown. |
| OQ-02 | On which day do the 75 leads arrive, and do they arrive together or spread across the week? | It sets the pull cadence, the batch size assumption, and whether a mid-week top-up is a normal event or an anomaly. A weekly close depends on knowing when the week's delivery is complete. | Office principal | P0 | Assume all 75 arrive together on Monday between 07:00 and 09:00 IST. `arrived_at` is stored per lead so a spread delivery needs no code change. Pulls run at 08:00 and 16:00 to cover both cases. |
| OQ-03 | Does the portal export include buyer phone, buyer email, full inquiry text and the arrival timestamp? | It determines whether `contact_missing` can fire at all, whether the drafts can be addressed, and whether the arrival-to-contact KPI is computable. A missing timestamp makes the 30-minute gate unmeasurable. | Office principal, verified in P0 against a real export | P0, before P3 | Assume the export contains inquiry text, buyer name, buyer company, destination, quantity and arrival timestamp, and does **not** contain phone or email. The pipeline treats phone and email as optional, and `contact_missing` fires only when no channel at all is present. If the timestamp is absent, the ingest time is stored as `arrived_at` and the KPI is labelled "approximate". |
| OQ-04 | Does an authorised IndiaMART API exist for the seller account, and does a webhook or push notification exist? | If neither exists, polling and CSV import are the only path, and the API adapter is never built. If a webhook exists, the pull cadence simplifies and the 30-minute contact target becomes easier. | Office principal, verified in P0 with the account manager | P0 | Assume neither exists. The CSV export adapter and the manual entry adapter are built first and are the production default. The API adapter is written only after P0 confirms it, and it never becomes the only path. |
| OQ-05 | How large is the sales team, and what is the assignment rule — round robin, by country, by product, or by whoever is free? | It determines whether assignment is automatic or manual, whether the owner field is mandatory at ingest, and whether the workload view is needed in v1. A team of one makes the whole assignment subsystem unnecessary. | Office principal | P4 | Assume one operator and two sales executives. Assignment defaults to round robin across the active executives within a band, and every lead must have an owner before it can leave `ready`. A manual reassignment always overrides and is audit-logged. |
| OQ-06 | Can quotes and orders be imported later — as a monthly CSV, an export from the accounting system, or not at all — to build a contact-to-order funnel? | It is the only route to outcome labels, and therefore the only route to conversion insight, learned ranking and a defensible cost per order. Without it, the product's ceiling is triage quality. | Office principal | Post-P7, before any v2 planning | Assume a monthly CSV export is possible. The `lead_outcomes` table shape is defined in v1 but never populated and never reported on, so the later import needs no schema change. No v1 code path reads a column that is always null. |
| OQ-07 | Which destination countries are proven sellers, which are allowed but unproven, which are restricted, and which are blocked? | It is the entire input to the destination component (20 points) and to two hard gates. Without it, every country is `unknown`, nothing can reach `act_now`, and the destination rules are fabricated. | Office principal | P0, before P2 ships the country table | Assume every country is `unknown` except those the Office principal names in P0. `unknown` scores 0 and cannot reach `act_now`, which is deliberately conservative: the system under-recommends rather than recommending a destination the business cannot serve. |
| OQ-08 | What retention period is acceptable for buyer contact data, and are there contractual or legal constraints on storing it outside the portal? | It sets the purge job, the anonymisation rule and the AI egress decision. A contract that forbids local storage of contact details would block the encrypted-PII design entirely. | Office principal | P1, before the PII schema is finalised | Assume 180 days after last activity, then anonymise; inquiry text 365 days; assessments and audit 3 years (PRD Amendment Section 13.10). If the contract forbids local contact storage, contact fields are not stored at all and the drafts are addressed through the portal only. |
| OQ-09 | Which email provider or mailbox should the system use for operator alerts and notifications? | It determines the mail transport, the deliverability of the daily digest and the alert path. The in-app channel works regardless, but email is the only channel that reaches the operator when they are not looking at the app. | Office principal, Administrator | P1 | Assume the office's existing mailbox over SMTP, used only for operator-facing alerts and the daily digest. No buyer-facing email is ever sent by the system, so deliverability matters only for internal alerts. |
| OQ-10 | Is HTTPS required, and will anyone need to reach the system from outside the office LAN? | It decides whether TLS is terminated on the host, whether `SESSION_SECURE_COOKIE` can be true, and whether a VPN is required. It is also the difference between a LAN-only security posture and an internet-facing one. | Office principal | P1 | Assume no HTTPS and no remote access in v1. The host binds to the LAN only, `SESSION_SECURE_COOKIE=false`, and the port is never forwarded. If remote access is later required, it goes through the office VPN and TLS is terminated on the host before anyone is given access. |
| OQ-11 | Is it acceptable to send inquiry text to a third-party AI provider at all, and if so, with what redaction? | It gates every model phase in v1.1. A refusal means the only usable provider is local Ollama, which changes the hardware requirement and the quality ceiling. A conditional yes means the redaction rules in PRD Amendment Section 13.11 become the contract. | Office principal, Developer | Before v1.1 M4 starts | Assume no third-party egress. v1 makes no model call at all, so nothing is blocked today. For v1.1 the default is local Ollama; Gemini is enabled only after a written egress decision, and redaction is applied regardless of provider. |

**Open-question discipline.** An unanswered question never blocks a phase, because every question has a default. The default is written into the code and into the UI label, so a downstream reader can see which assumption was in force. When an answer arrives, it is recorded here with the date, the answering role, and the decision it produced, and any default that was in force is retired in the same change.

## 5. What would make us stop

These are the conditions under which the project ends or is suspended, rather than being fixed. They exist so that the decision to stop is made on evidence and not on exhaustion.

1. **There is no lawful route to get the 75 leads into the system.** If P0 establishes that no authorised feed exists, that the export is unusable, and that manually entering 75 leads a week is not viable for the team, then the product cannot ingest its own input and the build stops at P0.

2. **The reseller contract ends, or the allowance drops below a level where triage is worth the effort.** Fifty leads a week still justifies the system; a handful does not. The threshold is a business judgement, not a technical one, but it must be stated before the build starts, not discovered after.

3. **The portal terms forbid using the data in an independent system.** If the terms prohibit storage or processing outside the portal, every part of this product is non-compliant and no amount of engineering fixes it.

4. **Product-match accuracy stays below 80 per cent after the catalogue and synonym work, and the operator judges the top candidate wrong more than one time in five.** At that point the score is not imperfect, it is actively misleading, and it would make the operator slower than reading the portal by eye.

5. **Hard-gate precision cannot reach 100 per cent, and every lead would need a manual gate review.** The gates exist to remove work, not to add it. If every lead needs a human to check the gates, the product has inverted its own purpose.

6. **The business cannot answer OQ-07 or OQ-01.** Without proven-seller countries, the destination component and two of the hard gates are invented. Without the fee, the waste metric is a count, not a cost, and the operator's spend-visibility pain is unanswered. Inventing either would make every number in the product untrustworthy.

7. **A data-protection obligation makes local storage of buyer contact details unlawful**, and no compliant posture (no stored contact fields, drafts addressed through the portal only) preserves enough value to justify the build.

8. **The team will not log contacts for four consecutive weeks.** Utilisation, arrival-to-contact and agreement are all derived from the contact log. Without it the product cannot demonstrate that it helped, and an unmeasurable product should not keep consuming a developer's time.

9. **The weekly allowance is consistently delivered late or short by the portal, for reasons the system cannot influence, and the Office principal will not escalate it.** The system can only triage what it receives. A supply problem upstream is a contract problem, and the right response is a conversation with the portal, not more code.

10. **The operator stops using the inbox and returns to reading the portal directly.** This is the clearest possible signal. It means the ranked inbox is slower, less trustworthy or less complete than the thing it replaced, and the correct response is to stop and find out why, not to add features.

**On stopping.** Stopping is not failure; continuing to build something the business does not use is. If any condition above is met, write it down with the evidence, record a decision in Section 4 that supersedes the relevant earlier decision, and stop. The six documents that exist by then — this one, the PRD amendment, the architecture, the evaluation dataset, the test suite and the audit trail — remain the record of what was tried and what was learned.
